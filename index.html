The rules, in brief

  • Who files. Voice providers, gateway providers, and other intermediate providers. VoIP resellers and MVNOs that meet the definitions file too.
  • What you need first. A business-type FRN from CORES. An individual FRN will not submit.
  • STIR/SHAKEN. “Complete” means the whole network is IP. Anything less needs a current exemption. The old small-provider extensions expired in 2022 and 2023.
  • People. Name at least one principal. That person is an individual.

Numbers and duties: DA 26-72, FCC 24-135, RMD FAQ.

FAQ

The details, if you want them.

Who has to file in the Robocall Mitigation Database?

Voice service providers and intermediate providers, including gateway providers, must file. That includes VoIP resellers and MVNOs that meet those definitions. An affiliate or subsidiary that independently meets the definition submits its own filing. A voice service provider furnishes voice to an end user over the public switched telephone network using North American Numbering Plan numbers. An intermediate provider carries or processes that traffic and neither originates nor terminates it. A gateway provider is a U.S.-based intermediate provider that receives a call directly from a foreign provider at its U.S. facilities. The definitions are in 47 CFR § 64.6300.

What do I need before I can submit a filing?

An FCC username and a business-type FCC Registration Number from CORES. The Robocall Mitigation Database form cannot be submitted with an individual-type FRN. The business name and address on the form come from CORES and are changed there, not on the form. CORES information has to be updated within 10 business days of a change.

When is the annual recertification due?

A provider with an existing filing recertifies on or before March 1 each year that the information in the database is true and correct. The recertification window opens on February 1. Filers also update a filing within 10 business days of a change to the information in it. The Commission adopted a $100 fee for an initial submission and for each annual recertification. As of the Wireline Competition Bureau’s January 22, 2026 public notice, that fee was not yet effective. Routine updates do not carry it.

What happens if a provider is not in the database?

FCC rules permit intermediate providers and voice service providers to accept traffic directly only from providers whose filings appear in the Robocall Mitigation Database. A deficient filing can be removed. The base forfeiture for failing to update changed information within 10 business days is $1,000 per violation, and it continues until cured. The base forfeiture for false or inaccurate information is $10,000 per violation, continuing until cured, up to the statutory maximum. Both amounts took effect on February 5, 2026.

Does RMD Autopilot file with the FCC?

RMD Autopilot prepares your filing. An officer of your company signs and submits it in the FCC portal. It does not guarantee that the FCC will accept the filing or that other providers will carry your traffic.
