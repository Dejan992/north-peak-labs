RMD Autopilot

Keep an FCC Robocall Mitigation Database filing current.

For small voice providers.

RMD Autopilot is a tool North Peak Labs is building to help a small voice provider collect what an RMD filing asks for, keep the mitigation plan with it, and remember the annual recertification. It does not sign or submit the filing to the FCC.

02 — The obligation

What the FCC actually requires.

A short account of the Robocall Mitigation Database rules, from the Commission’s own notices. This is not legal advice, and it is not a promise that a particular filing will be accepted.

Who files

Voice service providers and intermediate providers, including gateway providers, must file in the Robocall Mitigation Database. VoIP resellers and MVNOs that meet those definitions must file. An affiliate or subsidiary that independently meets the definition submits its own filing.

A voice service provider furnishes voice communications to an end user on a service interconnected with the public switched telephone network, using North American Numbering Plan numbers. An intermediate provider carries or processes traffic that traverses that network and neither originates nor terminates it. A gateway provider is a U.S.-based intermediate provider that receives a call directly from a foreign provider at its U.S. facilities before sending it on.

Before the form

A filing needs an FCC username and a business-type FCC Registration Number from CORES. An individual-type FRN cannot be used. The business name and address on the RMD form are filled from CORES and are read-only; changes are made in CORES.

Information in CORES has to be updated within 10 business days of a change. That rule took effect on February 5, 2026.

The filing itself

The form asks for each role the filer plays, whether it is a foreign voice service provider, and for principals, affiliates, subsidiaries, and parent companies. Every filer names at least one principal, and that principal must be an individual.

The filer certifies STIR/SHAKEN status. “Complete” is only for a network that is entirely IP-based. Partial or no implementation requires naming the extension or exemption that applies and explaining why. The extensions for non-facilities-based and facilities-based small voice service providers expired on June 30, 2022, and June 30, 2023, and cannot be used for that answer. The filing includes a robocall mitigation program description, and the FCC’s instructions require a signed declaration before the form is submitted.

After it is in

Filers update the RMD within 10 business days of a change to the information in the filing, including a change in ownership or control. The Commission’s base forfeiture for missing that update is $1,000 per violation, and the violation continues until it is cured. That forfeiture amount took effect on February 5, 2026.

Separately, a provider with an existing filing recertifies annually, on or before March 1, that the information is true and correct. The window opens on February 1. Under 47 CFR § 64.6305(h), which took effect February 5, 2026, the first window opened February 1, 2026, and those recertifications were due March 1, 2026. The same March 1 date applies each year.

The Commission adopted a $100 fee for an initial submission and for each required annual recertification. As of the Wireline Competition Bureau’s January 22, 2026 public notice, that fee was not yet effective. Routine updates to reflect a change do not carry that fee.

If it comes out

Intermediate providers and voice service providers may accept traffic directly only from a provider whose filing appears in the Robocall Mitigation Database. A deficient filing can be removed. The Commission’s base forfeiture for submitting false or inaccurate information to the RMD is $10,000 per violation, continuing until cured, up to the statutory maximum for a continuing violation. That amount took effect on February 5, 2026.

04 — FAQ

Short answers from the rules.

Who has to file in the Robocall Mitigation Database?

Voice service providers and intermediate providers, including gateway providers, must file. That includes VoIP resellers and MVNOs that meet those definitions. An affiliate or subsidiary that independently meets the definition submits its own filing. The definitions are in 47 CFR § 64.6300.

What do I need before I can submit a filing?

An FCC username and a business-type FCC Registration Number from CORES. The Robocall Mitigation Database form cannot be submitted with an individual-type FRN. The business name and address on the form come from CORES and are changed there, not on the form.

When is the annual recertification due?

A provider with an existing filing recertifies on or before March 1 each year that the information in the database is true and correct. The recertification window opens on February 1. Filers also update a filing within 10 business days of a change to the information in it.

What happens if a provider is not in the database?

FCC rules permit intermediate providers and voice service providers to accept traffic directly only from providers whose filings appear in the Robocall Mitigation Database. A deficient filing can be removed. The Commission can also impose forfeitures for false or inaccurate information, or for failing to update changed information within 10 business days.

Does RMD Autopilot file with the FCC?

No. The product is not live. It is being built to help a provider organize the details a filing asks for and remember recertification and update deadlines. An officer still signs the declaration in the FCC portal. RMD Autopilot does not guarantee that a filing will be accepted or that other providers will carry traffic.
